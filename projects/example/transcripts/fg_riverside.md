# Focus group: Riverside savings group (fictional)

MODERATOR: Thank you all for coming. To start, can you tell me what makes it easier for you to save every week?

PARTICIPANT 1: For me it is the meeting itself. When you know that on Thursday everyone will see whether you brought your contribution, you find a way. Nobody wants to be the one who comes with empty hands.

PARTICIPANT 2: Yes, and the treasurer calls your name out loud. [laughter] So you save.

PARTICIPANT 3: Also, before the group I kept money in the house and my husband or the children would find it. Now it is in the box and it has three keys.

MODERATOR: And what makes it harder to save?

PARTICIPANT 2: The dry season. When there is no harvest I do casual work, and some weeks there is no work at all. Those weeks I cannot bring the full amount.

PARTICIPANT 4: For me it was when my mother was sick. All the money went to the clinic for two months.

PARTICIPANT 1: Prices in the market also changed. Tomatoes I sell were very cheap last year, so I earned less.

MODERATOR: Let's talk about loans. Has anyone taken a loan from the group? What did you use it for?

PARTICIPANT 3: I took a loan to buy a second sewing machine. Now my daughter also sews and we finish the school uniforms faster, so we earn more.

PARTICIPANT 4: My loan was for the clinic, when my mother was sick. Without the group I would have gone to the moneylender.

PARTICIPANT 2: I used mine for school fees at the start of the term. It is the hardest time of the year.

MODERATOR: Besides money, has the group changed anything else for you?

PARTICIPANT 1: We are like sisters now. When my child was in hospital the women came to visit and brought food.

PARTICIPANT 4: I learned many things from the others, how to keep records for my small shop, how to talk to suppliers.

PARTICIPANT 3: Sometimes it is a lot of pressure also. If you are late with repayment everybody knows, and you feel ashamed to come to the meeting.
